2.2 Step 1: Check Variable Form

We want to predict CHD risk (TenYearCHD) from age. First, inspect the empirical CHD probability across age.

framingham |>
  mutate(CHD_numeric = as.numeric(TenYearCHD) - 1) |>
  ggplot(aes(x = age, y = CHD_numeric)) +
  geom_jitter(alpha = 0.06, height = 0.04, color = "steelblue") +
  geom_smooth(method = "loess", color = "firebrick", se = TRUE, linewidth = 1) +
  labs(x = "Age (years)",
       y = "Probability of CHD within 10 years",
       title = "Empirical CHD Probability by Age") +
  theme_minimal()

The loess curve rises steadily from low risk in younger participants to higher risk in older participants. A logistic regression with age as a linear term on the log-odds scale looks appropriate.

2.3 Step 2: Fit the Model and Interpret

chd_age <- glm(TenYearCHD ~ age,
               data = framingham,
               family = binomial)

The coefficient on age is in log-odds units. To get the odds ratio:

exp(cbind(OR = coef(chd_age), confint(chd_age)))

(Intercept) 0.003844584 0.002190705 0.006664767

Each additional year of age multiplies the odds of CHD by about 1.10. Rescaled to a decade:

exp(10 * coef(chd_age)["age"])
     age 
2.109606 
exp(10 * confint(chd_age)["age", ])
   2.5 %   97.5 % 
1.904065 2.340691

A 10-year age difference is associated with roughly a 2.5-fold increase in the odds of CHD over 10 years of follow-up.

Predicted probability of CHD rises from about 7% at age 35 to about 28% at age 65.

framingham <- framingham |>
  mutate(p_age = predict(chd_age, type = "response"))

roc_age <- roc(framingham$TenYearCHD, framingham$p_age,
               levels = c("No","Yes"), direction = "<")
auc(roc_age)
Area under the curve: 0.6792

The AUC for age alone is around 0.68. Age is a reasonable single predictor but far from perfect. Adding more predictors should improve discrimination.

4.3 Fitting Models

Function What it does
glm(y ~ x, data = df, family = binomial) Simple logistic regression
glm(y ~ x1 + x2, data = df, family = binomial) Multiple logistic regression
summary(model) Coefficients, z-tests, deviance
coef(model) Just the estimated coefficients
confint(model) 95% CIs for coefficients (on log-odds scale)
exp(coef(model)) Odds ratios
exp(confint(model)) 95% CIs for ORs
exp(cbind(OR = coef(m), confint(m))) OR table in one go
AIC(m1, m2) Compare models, lower is better
anova(m_small, m_large, test = "LRT") Likelihood ratio test for nested models
step(model, direction = "both", trace = 0) AIC-based stepwise selection

4.7 Rescaling Odds Ratios to Meaningful Units

# For a continuous predictor, the per-1-unit OR is often uninterpretable.
# Multiply the coefficient by the meaningful unit before exponentiating.
exp(10 * coef(model)["age"])           # 10-year OR
exp(20 * confint(model)["sysBP", ])    # 20 mmHg CI for sysBP
exp(40 * coef(model)["totChol"])       # 40 mg/dL OR

4.8 Common Interpretive Phrases

Quantity How to say it
OR = 1.5 “1.5-fold higher odds” or “50% higher odds”
OR = 2.0 “double the odds”
OR = 0.5 “half the odds” or “50% lower odds”
OR = 1.0 (CI includes 1) “no evidence of an association”
Adjusted OR “after controlling for [other variables]”
